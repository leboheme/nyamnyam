# Nyam Nyam — Game Design

This document describes *Nyam Nyam* from the player’s point of view. It is a reconstruction of the game as implemented in the current code and assets, rather than a record of the original design intent.

## High concept

Nyam Nyam is a food-themed, single-screen platform-action game. The player helps a small creature clear a series of arenas by defeating enemies, turning them into candy, and eating everything left behind.

The core loop is:

> move and jump → attack enemies → transform or defeat them → eat the resulting food → clear the level

The intro suggests that Nyam is born from an egg and needs help eating. The world is playful and food-oriented: enemies become candy, ordinary rewards are fruit and vegetables, and many upgrades are foods.

## Player objective

Each level is cleared by removing all enemies. Some attacks immediately defeat an enemy; Nyam’s normal beam can instead transform an enemy into a piece of candy. The candy must then be eaten before the level can finish.

When the arena is clear, the game gives the player a short completion delay and advances to the next level. If the player loses all lives, the game ends.

There are 60 numbered levels. Boss encounters appear in levels 20, 35, 50, and 60; a separate `boss.tmx` map also exists as a boss-stage template or unused special stage.

## Controls

The game was designed for touch controls, with a virtual gamepad:

- Left side: move left and right.
- Right side: jump and use the character’s attack.

Desktop keyboard controls are:

- Left/right arrows: move.
- Up arrow: jump.
- Space: attack.
- Escape/back: pause or resume.
- Tab: toggle fullscreen/windowed mode.

Movement, jumping, attacks, and collisions are real-time. Levels are fixed arenas rather than scrolling exploration spaces.

## Playable characters

The player can maintain separate progress for three characters and select the active character from the game menu.

### Nyam

Nyam is the default character and the most direct shooter.

- Moves and jumps normally.
- Fires a horizontal beam.
- Can use air objects as platforms without requiring the extra-air assistance used by the other characters.
- Is the clearest expression of the shoot–transform–eat loop.

### Gluty

Gluty is a close-range character.

- Attacks by spinning into enemies.
- Can defeat enemies by contact while spinning.
- Has a double-jump-like aerial attack behavior.
- Benefits especially from upgrades that extend or improve the spin.

### Tfly

Tfly is an explosive character.

- Throws bombs rather than firing a straight beam.
- Bombs travel in an arc and explode after a delay.
- Can receive wider explosions and longer explosion timing through upgrades.
- Also has a double jump.

The characters are not merely cosmetic variants: their attack range, timing, movement during attacks, and relationship with air platforms change how a level is approached.

## Enemies

The regular enemy roster contains:

- Boof
- Phant
- Rinos
- Lito
- Foll
- Mber
- Banqui

Their behaviors include walking, jumping, bouncing, climbing, invading the player’s space, and firing projectiles. Examples include Foll firing energy, Lito throwing rocks, and Rinos throwing horns.

Enemies can become angry when a level takes too long. Angry enemies move faster. After the normal-level timer becomes critical, a Dark enemy can enter the arena and pressure the player to finish quickly.

## Transforming and eating enemies

This is the game’s defining mechanic.

Some friendly attacks transform an enemy into candy instead of destroying it. The candy rises into the arena, can interact with air objects, and eventually becomes loose if left alone. The player must reach it and eat it.

Eating candy:

- removes the transformed enemy from the level’s remaining objective;
- awards points;
- can chain into nearby candy or air objects;
- produces the characteristic candy-eating feedback.

Garlic temporarily scares enemies. Scared enemies stop behaving normally and become edible on contact, allowing the player to eat them directly.

Bosses are different: they have their own life counters, cannot be eaten, and must be damaged repeatedly. Bosses periodically provide a special upgrade and release fruit when defeated.

## Food and collectibles

### Ordinary food

The game contains vegetables and fruit:

- Lettuce
- Cabbage
- Carrot
- Broccoli
- Apple
- Banana
- Cherries
- Orange
- Pineapple

Ordinary food is primarily a scoring and eating reward. Fruit can also appear when regular enemies are defeated normally.

### Candy

Candy is generated by transforming enemies. Its visual variants include bears, clouds, liquorice, paper, and sticks. Candy is more important than ordinary food because transformed candy is part of the level-clear condition.

### Air

Air objects rise through the arena and provide temporary platforms or hazards. They may be:

- ordinary air bubbles;
- tornado air;
- spike air.

Air objects can be jumped on and eaten. Eating special air can release an attack: tornado air creates a tornado projectile, while spike air creates a bouncing/spreading spike attack.

Some levels guide air along left and right paths authored in the level map. Other levels use automatic air behavior.

## Boosts

Boosts are edible temporary powerups. Most last for three lives rather than for a fixed number of seconds.

| Boost | Effect |
|---|---|
| Pizza | Increases movement speed. |
| Burger | Reduces the delay between attacks. |
| Meat | Increases attack distance or effective range. |
| Hotdog | Increases projectile or attack speed. |
| Live | Adds a life. |
| Caterpillar | Adds Tfly as a companion pet. |
| Egg | Adds Gluty as a companion pet. |
| Garlic | Scares enemies, making them vulnerable to being eaten. |
| Chilli | Gives Nyam and related characters a fireball attack. |
| Tornado | Gives a powerful tornado/beam-style special attack. |

The store sells several of the persistent three-life upgrades and bundles them into a “pro shooter” combination.

## Companions

Some boosts summon Gluty or Tfly as pets while the player controls another character. Companions are an extension of the player’s attack capabilities and are part of the game’s fantasy of the three creatures helping one another.

## Lives, score, and persistence

A new game begins with three lives. Losing a life reduces active upgrade durations as well as the life count. The game can grant additional lives through:

- life boosts;
- reaching score thresholds;
- daily gifts or rewarded video features;
- store purchases.

Score comes from eating food, eating candy, defeating enemies, and eating air objects. Reaching score milestones can grant an extra life.

Progress is saved locally per character, including level, lives, score, and active upgrades. This makes the game a light campaign rather than a purely run-based arcade game.

## Level rhythm

A typical level follows this rhythm:

1. The player enters a compact platform arena.
2. Enemies and placed food define the immediate challenge.
3. Food spawns into the level gradually rather than appearing all at once.
4. Air objects create moving platform opportunities and hazards.
5. The player attacks enemies and decides whether to defeat or transform them.
6. After roughly one minute on a normal level, the game warns the player to hurry and enrages enemies.
7. After roughly 75 seconds, Dark may appear if the level is not already under control.
8. The player eats remaining candy and clears the arena.

Boss levels remove the normal hurry/Dark pressure and focus on repeated damage, survival, and special boss rewards.

## Player experience and identity

The intended emotional loop seems to be playful accumulation: movement creates opportunities to eat, attacks turn threats into food, and food makes the character more capable. The game’s strongest identity comes from the unusual conversion of combat into appetite:

> enemies are not simply destroyed; they become something the player wants to chase.

That mechanic gives the original game a strong foundation for a remake. Additional complexity should ideally deepen the relationships between enemies, candy, air, food, and the three characters rather than replace the central shoot–transform–eat rhythm.
